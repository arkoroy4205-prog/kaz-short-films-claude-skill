# Story formula

The three reference films share a seven-beat structure. Timings below describe an approximately 2:30 film; scale proportionally for other lengths.

| # | Beat | Share | What happens | Typical devices |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Loop | 0–10% | The hero fails at a recurring anchor moment. | black caption card, bedroom, clock, small DAY label |
| 2 | Enemy appears | 10–30% | The shadow appears and whispers; day jumps show time passing. | couch, desk, bed, red whispers |
| 3 | Identity attack | 30–35% | The shadow appears in the mirror and attacks the hero's identity. | mirror, huge red whisper, impact cue |
| 4 | Rock bottom | 35–40% | The hero is isolated in a spotlight; the narration becomes quiet. | spotlight, silence |
| 5 | Reframe / turn | 40–45% | A new interpretation and a concrete trigger begin the change. | returning anchor, close-up, DAY 1 |
| 6 | The climb | 45–85% | Repeated effort, day jumps, setbacks, and persistence. | running, training, stairs, progress meter |
| 7 | Summit + bookend + title | 85–100% | The hero reaches a symbolic summit, revisits the opening, then lands the thesis. | sunset, dawn bedroom, title card |

## Whisper ladder

1. Comfort: “just 5 more minutes…”, “you deserve it.”, “tomorrow.”
2. Doubt: “you're tired.”, “why even try?”
3. Identity: “you're lazy.”, “this is who you are.”
4. Isolation: “nobody cares.”, “nobody is watching.”
5. Final: “quit.”, “you can't.”

## Variant structures

- **Meter film:** show motivation rising and draining; the turn is acting without waiting to feel motivated. End with a visual contrast between spikes and a steady staircase.
- **Generational film:** use two time periods and a family conversation to show a harmful silence being broken. Speech bubbles can replace narration in intimate scenes.
